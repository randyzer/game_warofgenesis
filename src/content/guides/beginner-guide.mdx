War of Genesis becomes easier to reason about when you treat each session as a short decision loop: set a goal, run content reliably, inspect what changed, and then choose whether to push forward or consolidate.

## Start with one progression goal

Pick the constraint that matters now. You may be trying to unlock more content, strengthen a build, replace a weak equipment slot, or collect items worth checking on the Steam Community Market. A single goal makes drops and upgrades easier to evaluate.

Avoid spreading resources across every promising item. Keep a stable setup available while you test changes, so a failed experiment does not erase your reliable farming baseline.

## Learn the core loop

1. **Choose a goal.** Write down what a successful session should improve.
2. **Run content you can clear consistently.** Reliability gives you useful observations.
3. **Review gear against the goal.** Compare build fit and replacement difficulty, not rarity alone.
4. **Choose the next action.** Advance, stay, step back, or make a focused equipment change.
5. **Review again after a meaningful change.** A new item or build direction can move the best decision.

## Connect gear, farming, and the market

Gear has progression value before it has sale value. Ask whether an item improves your current goal, is difficult to replace, or supports a future setup. Only then decide whether it deserves a manual market check.

Steam Wallet proceeds are not the same as the buyer-facing price. If you consider listing an eligible item, use the [Steam Market guide](/market/) to understand the workflow and the [fee calculator](/tools/steam-market-fee-calculator/) to estimate the fee split.

## What to understand before optimizing

- A dependable clear can be more useful than an inconsistent push.
- A build direction should match the activity you care about, not a context-free tier label.
- Marketability must be checked in the current Steam interface for the specific item.
- Current prices and future demand are not guaranteed by this wiki.
- Your own repeatable observations are more useful than an unsourced “best stage” claim.
