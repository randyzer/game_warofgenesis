This War of Genesis: Idle Loot guide starts with the game's confirmed loop: leave the Windows desktop client running, let your character fight automatically, and use the equipment that drops to develop your character. Blacksmith functions handle combining, dismantling, and jewels. Start with those systems before treating an old class ranking or farming route as a rule for the October release.

<h2 id="desktop-loop">Understand the desktop loot loop</h2>

The official Steam description presents a transparent desktop character that battles while the game is running. Your character can keep fighting while you work or sleep, with weapons, armor, and jewels among the drops. This describes background play with an active client; it does not establish rewards after closing the game.

Equipment growth sits alongside Alliance Raid, World Boss, PvP Arena, and eligible-item trading through the Steam Community Market. These are separate parts of the game. An item dropping in battle does not by itself establish that it can be sold, and a market listing does not tell you how quickly you will earn another copy.

For the product's platform and release context, read the [game overview](/guides/). It distinguishes this Steam title from the similarly named Idle Heroes mobile game.

<h2 id="blacksmith">Know what each Blacksmith function does</h2>

September 16 Early Access guides describe three Equipment Blacksmith functions: Combine, Dismantle, and Jewel. Combine handles equipment synthesis; Dismantle breaks equipment down; Jewel handles socketing. These names explain different operations rather than three interchangeable upgrade buttons.

Those guides also describe automatic equipment combining with level and rarity filters. Their descriptions are useful background, but their exact menu layouts and unlock requirements were not rechecked in the release client. Read the current in-game action and selected items before committing resources. This guide does not supply unverified material counts, success probabilities, or failure refunds.

Keeping other-class weapons and unused jewels in reserve was a September author recommendation for players considering a later change. It is a strategy suggestion, not an official requirement or proof that a particular switching route remains unchanged.

<h2 id="choose-class">Choose a class without relying on a universal tier list</h2>

Knight, Ranger, and Wizard are the three names used in the October 7 official update. Older beginner guides disagree about the best starting choice: one recommends Ranger, while another recommends starting with Wizard and considering Ranger later. Their advice comes from different Early Access experiences.

Use the [classes reference](/guides/classes/) to separate confirmed names from those opinions. No equal-level, equal-equipment comparison in the supplied research establishes one best starter for every player. A recommendation for ordinary PvE also does not establish the best setup for Arena or World Boss.

An Early Access guide suggests returning to a stage you can clear consistently when progression stalls. Treat that as an option to evaluate in your own session, not a guaranteed optimal farming route. No hourly-yield calculation is published here.

<h2 id="release-context">Account for the October release</h2>

The October 7 update introduced G-Coins, skins, and equipment artifacts. A September menu list therefore cannot be treated as a complete inventory of the release version. The official news page is the place to compare dated changes with what the current client displays.

Next, use the [Steam Market reference](/guides/market/) if your question concerns trading rather than progression. This guide intentionally stops before a full leveling route, precise unlock table, or optimized build: those need evidence beyond the basic loop and menu functions documented here.
