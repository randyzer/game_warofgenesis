A crew is a set of answers, not a collection of rarities. Emberdeep repeatedly asks who can reveal danger, who can endure it, who can solve the obstacle, and who can keep the group steady afterward. Cover those questions first; optimize traits and signature gear once the rotation survives ordinary trouble.

<h2 id="four-expedition-roles">Four expedition roles</h2>

The **point** reveals chambers and manages route risk. The **anchor** takes exposed positions and prevents threats from reaching fragile members. The **specialist** handles the route's expected obstacle, such as mechanisms, spores, or armored creatures. The **keeper** protects Resolve, supplies, and recovery options. These are responsibilities rather than fixed classes. A watchful shield-bearer can be both point and anchor, while a medic carrying survey flares may share keeper duties with the scout.

Give every essential responsibility a backup. The backup can be weaker; it only needs to keep one injury or blocked action from collapsing the expedition. When choosing between two recruits, prefer the one who closes an uncovered job over the one who slightly improves a job already handled well.

<h2 id="three-crew-rotation">Three-crew rotation</h2>

Maintain one active crew, one recovery crew, and one ready crew. After the active crew returns, it moves to treatment or station work. The ready crew takes the next planned route, while the recovered crew becomes the new reserve. This rhythm prevents a single difficult descent from turning into several cycles of waiting.

Do not distribute power perfectly evenly. Give the active route the specialist it needs, but keep each other crew functional with shared gear and broad techniques. Name crews by purpose instead of strength: Lantern, Salvage, and Gate are more useful labels than First, Second, and Third because they remind you what supplies and roles each formation expects.

<h2 id="early-formations">Early formations</h2>

Start with an anchor in the front slot, the point beside or just behind them, and the keeper in the most protected position. Place the specialist where their tool can reach its target without exposing them to the first hazard. Change this default when a forecast gives a reason, such as rear attacks or a mechanism that must be reached immediately.

Test one formation change at a time in a familiar layer. If you swap positions, equipment, and crew members together, a good result teaches very little. Record whether the change preserved Resolve or reduced supply use across two runs. For the broader expedition rhythm that makes these crews useful, continue with [How to Play Emberdeep](/guides/how-to-play/).
