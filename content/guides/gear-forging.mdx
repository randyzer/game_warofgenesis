Gear turns a recruit's role into a dependable action. The Forge can improve that action, but every job consumes material, time, and Heat that another crew may need. Read equipment by the problem it solves, then spend enough to make the solution reliable. Chasing every green number produces a crowded storehouse and an unavailable repair queue.

<h2 id="read-a-gear-card">Read a gear card</h2>

Begin with the action line: what can the item do during an expedition? Next read its reach and slot requirement, because an excellent action in the wrong position may never fire. Durability shows how many stressful uses it tolerates before penalties appear. Weight affects the whole crew's supply allowance, not only the carrier. Finally, tags connect equipment to techniques, station projects, and hazard counters.

Compare cards in route context. A heavy breaker is valuable against repeated rubble but wasteful on a mapping run. A modest lantern with a certainty tag may save more Resolve than a brighter model when the Archive has already decoded the layer. Keep a small set of flexible tools that can move between crews without retraining.

<h2 id="upgrade-priorities">Upgrade priorities</h2>

Upgrade bottlenecks before strengths. If an anchor already survives comfortably, another armor level changes little; improving the keeper's supply efficiency may extend every descent. Favor durability when repairs block rotation, reach when formation forces awkward positions, and action reliability when a failed check creates cascading danger.

Plan Forge jobs around crew returns. Complete essential repairs first, then shared tools, then specialist improvements. Leave enough Heat capacity to accept one unexpected repair. Rare material belongs in equipment you expect to use across several objectives, unless a narrow upgrade is the clear key to a layer gate described in [World Progress](/guides/world-progress/).

<h2 id="recycle-safely">Recycle safely</h2>

Before recycling, reserve one working item for each common answer: light, treatment, protection, mechanisms, and material clearing. Check all three crews and the Forge queue; an item shown in storage may already be promised to a returning team. Lock relics and gear with unusual tags until the Archive reveals their interactions.

Recycle duplicates in small batches, then assign the recovered material immediately. This prevents a large salvage total from becoming permission for unrelated upgrades. If an old item fills no current role, supports no planned technique, and costs too much to repair, it is a safe candidate. The goal is not an empty storehouse. It is a storehouse where every retained piece has a foreseeable job.
